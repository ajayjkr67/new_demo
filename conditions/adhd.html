What is ADHD?

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interfere with functioning and development. It begins in childhood but in many people continues into adulthood — often undiagnosed.

ADHD is not a discipline problem, laziness, or bad parenting. It reflects genuine differences in how the brain manages attention, impulse control, and executive function.

Three presentations

  • Predominantly Inattentive — difficulty sustaining attention, easily distracted, forgetful, loses things, doesn't follow through on tasks. Less visible than hyperactive type and frequently missed, particularly in girls.
  • Predominantly Hyperactive-Impulsive — fidgeting, inability to stay seated, excessive talking, difficulty waiting, acts without thinking. More visible and more commonly diagnosed in younger children.
  • Combined Presentation — symptoms of both inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity. The most common presentation.

ADHD in adults

Adult ADHD often looks different from childhood ADHD. Hyperactivity may reduce, but inattention, disorganisation, poor time management, difficulty prioritising tasks, emotional dysregulation, and impulsivity often persist. Adults with undiagnosed ADHD frequently describe years of underperformance, frustration, and being told they're not living up to their potential.

Assessment

A thorough ADHD assessment involves a clinical interview covering developmental history, current symptoms across multiple settings, and ruling out other conditions (anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, and learning disabilities can all mimic ADHD symptoms). Rating scales and, where appropriate, neuropsychological testing may be used.

Treatment

Medication — stimulant medications (methylphenidate) are the most effective treatment for ADHD. They work quickly and have a well-established safety profile when used under medical supervision. Non-stimulant alternatives are available for patients who don't tolerate stimulants.

Behavioural therapy — particularly helpful for children; teaches skills for organisation, attention management, and behaviour regulation. Parent training programmes are an important component of childhood ADHD treatment.

Psychoeducation — understanding ADHD changes everything. Many adults with newly diagnosed ADHD describe relief at finally having an explanation for lifelong struggles.

For parents

If your child is struggling academically or behaviourally despite adequate effort, and teachers or family have observed poor attention or impulsivity across multiple settings, please seek a proper assessment. Early diagnosis and support make a significant difference to educational outcomes and self-esteem.
