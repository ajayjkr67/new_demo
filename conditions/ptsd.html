What is PTSD?

PTSD develops in some people following exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence. It represents the brain's threat-response system remaining activated long after the traumatic event has passed — as though the danger is still present.

Trauma can include combat, accidents, natural disasters, physical or sexual assault, sudden loss, medical emergencies, childhood abuse, or witnessing violence. The severity of the trauma does not always predict who develops PTSD — individual vulnerability, prior trauma history, and available support all play a role.

Symptoms

PTSD involves four clusters of symptoms:

  • Re-experiencing — flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories, psychological or physical distress when reminded of the trauma
  • Avoidance — avoiding thoughts, feelings, people, places, or situations associated with the trauma
  • Negative changes in cognition and mood — persistent negative beliefs about oneself or the world, distorted blame, persistent negative emotions (fear, horror, guilt, shame), diminished interest in activities, feeling detached or estranged
  • Hyperarousal — irritability or angry outbursts, reckless behaviour, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, concentration problems, sleep disturbance

Complex PTSD

Complex PTSD (C-PTSD) develops from prolonged or repeated trauma — particularly childhood abuse, domestic violence, or prolonged captivity. In addition to standard PTSD symptoms, it involves severe difficulties with emotional regulation, self-perception, and relationships.

Treatment

Trauma-focused CBT and EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) have the strongest evidence for PTSD. Both involve processing the traumatic memory in a controlled, structured way that allows the brain to integrate it rather than keeping it as a live threat.

Medication — SSRIs reduce the intensity of PTSD symptoms and are used alongside therapy, particularly for severe cases.

Important

PTSD is not weakness. It is a normal brain response to an abnormal event — a response that got stuck. Treatment works. Many people with PTSD recover and go on to live full lives, even after severe trauma.
